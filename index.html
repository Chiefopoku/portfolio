About Me

As a nurse in Ghana, the infections that worried me most were the ones growing on implants. That question, why some surfaces are easier for microbes to colonise, took me from the ward to the lab.

I am completing an M.S. in Nanoscience at UNC Greensboro (May 2027), and next I want to study antimicrobial resistance in water, including how metals in mining-affected water may help it persist.

Research Interests

Microbial adhesion and surfaces

How surfaces, and the films that coat them, decide whether microbes attach. Adhesion is the first step toward biofilms and device infections.

Antimicrobial resistance in water

How resistant bacteria and resistance genes persist and move through rivers, wastewater and reused water.

Metals and mining-affected water

Whether metals released by mining help keep antimicrobial resistance in circulation in rivers and groundwater.

Healthcare surfaces and disinfection

How fungal pathogens such as Candida auris persist on hospital surfaces, and what partial disinfection does to them.

From measurement to public health risk

Turning lab and field measurements into estimates of risk that can guide monitoring, regulation and policy.

One Health and Ghana

Linking clinical and environmental resistance, and helping build environmental surveillance capacity in Ghana.

Current Research

Conditioned surfaces

Does material secreted by Candida onto a surface change how bacteria attach to it?

Disinfectant exposure

Does low-level exposure to cleaning agents change how Candida auris attaches to surfaces?
